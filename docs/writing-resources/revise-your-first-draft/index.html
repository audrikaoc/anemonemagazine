Revision

Revise Your First Draft

A twenty-minute revision routine with a worked example for short fiction.

Set aside twenty minutes and save a copy of your draft. Use the first half of the session to work on the scene and the second half to read and edit the sentences.

Four minutes for the opening

Read the first paragraph. Underline the earliest moment when your character wants something or faces a choice. Try starting there. Keep the background details the reader needs to understand that moment.

Six minutes for the scene

Write one sentence about what changes by the end of the scene. Mark the actions or decisions that cause the change. If a part feels flat, give the character one concrete thing to do before they can get what they want.

Five minutes for detail

Find one place where you named a feeling. Try showing it through a movement, sound, or object a reader can picture.

First draft
Mira was nervous when her name was called.

Revised draft
When her name came over the speaker, Mira folded her audition number until the paper tore.

The revision gives Mira a physical action and lets the reader infer how she feels. Try writing a second version with a different action and see what changes.

Five minutes to read aloud

Read the scene at speaking pace. Mark where you stumble or need to pause. Try one shorter sentence at a turning point and cut one detail that repeats information the reader already has. Finish by checking the last line for a clear action or image.

If you want feedback, ask a workshop reader where they first understood what your character wanted. When you’re ready to send the story out, use the first submission guide.
